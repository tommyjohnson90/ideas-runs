## Legal and regulatory reviewer

Rounds 1 and 2, 2026-11-23. Agenda item 1: free sites for about a dozen well-networked business owners, in return for referrals. This is not legal advice. It flags issues and the usual small-company handling, and it lists what is worth paying a lawyer to confirm.

### Bottom line

Every option the board is weighing is legally workable: free or founding sites, the $50 offered to anyone, pricing's earn-back, pay-on-launch. Each needs one page of written terms. The first free site is blocked only by:
- the written agreement
- the no-reviews/no-insurance rule
- conditions stated next to any "free" claim
- contractor registration numbers on contractor sites
- keeping Lauren apart

The first referral payout is blocked only by written referral terms, which must include an employee rule, since pricing names stylists as payees, and a payout log. Handling all of it costs about $0 to $750 (a guess).

### Changed after team review

I read all six teammate files (cfo, customer-advocate, contrarian, ops-risk, customer-experience, pricing-gtm) and the updated context.md. This round ran through files, so I sent no messages.

**What changed in my view, and why:**

1. **The employee rule matters more than I thought in round 1.** The advocate (motion 1), the CFO (motion 2), pricing (motion 5) and cx (motion 8) all want the $50 paid "to anyone". Pricing's file names the payees: "Each stylist gets $50 if their owner buys" (pricing-gtm.md, first-ten list item 2), alongside a landlord and an association director. Paying the buyer's own employee to steer the buyer is the commercial-bribery pattern in RI §§ 11-7-3/4 (a felony) and Mass. c. 271 § 39.
   - **Fix:** "to anyone" is fine, except that a person who works for the buying business is paid only if the buying owner knows in writing. The simplest way is for Tom to say it on the first touch ("Dana at your front desk sent me; I pay $50 to whoever introduces me") and log it.
   - **Landlords** are not the tenant's agent, so they're fine.
   - **An association director** is the association's agent. Pay only if the association's policy allows it, or make the offer to the association itself.
   - **Public employees acting in their job** (the Brattleboro Recreation director tied to the Winter Carnival, a town committee member): never pay them.
   - This blocks the **first payout**, not the first free site.
2. **Pricing's two-tier earn-back ($125, $125, then $50) and "Two neighbours who buy and yours is free"** are legal as designed. Two changes:
   - (a) **The counter line needs its condition in the same breath.** "Yours is free" said to someone about to pay $250 is a conditional "free" claim. The FTC's "free" guide (16 CFR 251, from memory) and Mass. 93A (which covers business buyers) expect the condition stated clearly up front. Suggested wording: "$250, done, yours. Send me two neighbours who buy and I'll pay you back $125 for each." The same goes for the advocate's "five referred buyers repay your $250". It blocks putting the line on the leave-behind card or popup.
   - (b) **Tax treatment differs.** A $125 refund to a paying customer, capped at what they paid, is most likely a price reduction on their own purchase, not income to report on a 1099 (inferred; a CPA should confirm). The $50 paid to a non-customer, or after the refunds, is referral income and counts toward the $2,000 1099-NEC threshold. The ledger has to tell them apart.
   - Single-level rewards only: never pay for referrals of referrals. That keeps it well clear of pyramid-scheme rules (inferred, no issue today).
   - The contrarian's "$25 off for the referred business" (motion 3) and cx's give-or-get (motion 9) are fine. Price-discrimination law (Robinson-Patman) covers goods, not services (from memory).
3. **cx's founding sites and per-lead popup.** The popup line "One of my first dozen sites: no charge. Ask me how." should state the conditions up front, not behind "ask me how". Suggested: "One of my first dozen sites: no charge, in return for a small footer credit and a few introductions." "First dozen" must also stay literally true. This blocks shipping the popup variant, not the free site itself.
4. **cx's footer credit ("Website by Tom · pvdsites.com", linking to a mailto).** A mailto link passes no search ranking, so my nofollow concern (row 12) is solved by cx's design. A plain-text "pvdsites.com" is also fine. Any normal link to pvdsites.com should carry rel="nofollow". On "required on founding sites" (cx) versus my "removable any time": I now accept a stated term, "kept for 12 months; tell me if you want it off sooner". A clear time limit keeps the barter value trivial and avoids a dispute. There is no legal objection to ops's "no credit on business sites" either; it is a business choice.
5. **cx's day-30 visit-count text.** This changes my "Tom removes his Cloudflare access at handoff". The agreement should instead say Tom keeps member access for 30 days after launch to fix errors and read the visit count, then removes himself unless the owner asks him to stay. Cloudflare Web Analytics is cookieless, so I see no consent banner duty in RI, MA, NH or VT (inferred, from memory). A one-line privacy note on the site is good practice. The text itself is manual, to a person who gave Tom their number, so it is not a cold text (D-2050) and the TCPA risk is low (from memory). Quote the real number only.
