## Contrarian

### Bottom line
Don't give free sites to businesses during the trial. Only about 5 or 6 owners have been pitched, nobody has raised a trust objection, and every demo popup has already shown "$250" to anyone who opened it. At the team's own estimates, a dozen free sites would take 45 to 110% (most likely about 70%) of Tom's roughly 110 remaining hours. Spend the next weeks on follow-up, the named-neighbour referral ask and an objection log. Give at most one free site, to a community group, after the first paid delivery.

### The three load-bearing assumptions and the case against each

**Assumption 1: the 0 sales are a trust problem that a giveaway fixes.**
This is the weakest of the three. The case against it:
- **The denominator is wrong.** "0 from 85 demos" counts demos built, not pitches made. The secretary's count from the logged interactions (I-153 to I-179, 11-18 to 11-21) is roughly 5 or 6 owners reached with a demo (current-state.md section 8, "Stage labels under-record contact"). Getting 0 closes from about 6 conversations in 4 days tells you nothing about trust.
- **The logged reasons are about reaching owners and following up, not trust.** Several visits found the owner "out or busy" (I-162 to I-164, I-168, I-175). Staff stood in for owners: two stylists said they would pass both demo links on (I-173, I-174), and a bartender said the owners were "not very involved" (I-171). One office was "all set" (I-167). Still Fly's objection was about branding ("isn't her branding", I-159), not trust. No price objection is logged, and no "who are you?" objection is logged either.
- **The one real proposal is stalling on Tom's side.** Lobanton's next action ("follow up with Judy for brand assets") was due 11-20 and is 3 days overdue. Its preview comes down 11-27, although under D-2052 it should run to about 12-02 (current-state.md section 8, inferred there). That is a follow-up problem, and a giveaway does nothing for it.
- **A trust gap may exist, but it sits in the payment step, not the site.** The offer asks a stranger to send $250 by Venmo or Apple Pay before anything is delivered (CLAUDE.reference.md line 14). Venmo's own guidance says to use the app only with people you know and to avoid paying strangers for goods or services. Buyer protection applies only to business profiles or payments tagged as goods and services (venmo.com/legal/us-helpful-information; summarized by McAfee and Proton). If trust is the issue, the cheap fix is to let owners pay once the site is live, or to send a business invoice. A free site is the expensive fix.
- **The fair counter-case.** GoDaddy's survey material says 64% of owners would be suspicious of a web pro with no reviews, and 60 to 73% find a designer through a recommendation (vendor survey, numbers differ between pages). PVD Sites has no live client site and no testimonial. So proof of work has real value. That argues for 2 or 3 reference sites, not 12, and it does not show that the current 0 is caused by missing proof.

**Assumption 2: "networked" owners can be identified, and they will refer.**
- **Identifying them is not a defined procedure.** Nothing in the repo or in Open Brain scores how networked an owner is. The lead scoring (D-2002) rewards "no or weak website, owner-run, not paying a designer." Inferred: the most connected owners (chamber board members, business-association officers, BNI regulars) are the ones most likely to already have a site or pay a designer, so the scoring filters them out. The best-connected candidates in the data are not businesses at all. One is the Greenfield Girls Softball League, whose 10 teams are each sponsored by a local business (docs/free-sites.md). The other is Lauren, who already sent one website referral (I-55) and has a list of 29 prospects (I-176).
- **Saying they'll refer is not the same as referring.** The "83% willing, 29% do" figure is everywhere in marketing material but has no traceable primary source. Treat it as unverified. The nearest real study (Advisor Impact, Schwab and Texas Tech, on financial-advisor clients) found 91% comfortable referring and 29% who actually did in the past 12 months. Sauro and Lewis found 81% said they would recommend and about 30% did (MeasuringU). Either way, a free-site recipient who says "I'll send people your way" should be expected, on base rates, to send nobody about 70% of the time.
- **The only real-world numbers come from a vendor and are weak.** The Free Website Guys say about 18% of their free-site recipients hire them or refer someone who does (a company claim reported in search results, not independently checked). Applied to 12 free sites, that is about 2 conversions. This is inferred arithmetic on an unverified rate.

**Assumption 3: the free site is the reason they would refer.**
- **Research says payment to the referrer adds little.** Gershon, Cryder and John (Journal of Marketing Research 57(1), 2020) ran a market survey of 351 programs plus eight experiments. Senders referred at about the same rate whether or not they were rewarded. Their motive is mostly reputational: referrals dropped sharply when they were anonymous. What moved results was a benefit to the person being referred, at the stage where that person decides to sign up. A free site is a very large reward to the referrer (worth $250 plus Tom's delivery hours). It pays for the wrong half of the referral.
- **Owners already refer without a free site.** Madison Hart of Still Fly Studios may introduce Tom to the Skittlez Ink owner, unpaid and before buying anything (I-165). That is one data point, but it is the only real referral evidence we have, and no gift was involved.
- **A free site can hurt the referral.** A neighbor who hears "he did mine for free" anchors on free, not on $250. Tom prospects in tight clusters (The Arcade is one building; plazas and main streets). Inferred: in clusters like these, word of a free deal travels faster than word of a good site.

### Pre-mortem
It is 2026-12-31. In the week after the board meeting, Tom picked twelve owners: a well-known barber, Descavich Plumbing (a softball-league sponsor), two people from the chamber lists, the Lobanton owner, and others. Nine said yes, as people do to anything free. Then the work started, and none of it was automated. A free client has no payment moment, so the D-1951 trigger never fired, and photos, logos and copy trickled in or never came. Google Business Profile verification held up three of the listing cleanups for weeks. Two owners wanted their domains bought, and nobody had written down who pays. By mid-December, five free sites were live and four were still waiting on content. Tom had spent about 70 of his roughly 110 trial hours on them (a guess at the CFO's 6.5 h a site; no delivery has been timed), during the same weeks as the yacht-ai paid launch. The live free sites looked good. When asked, the owners said "absolutely, I'll tell people." Three introductions came in. Two of them opened with "Mike said you did his for free." ### Fatal vs fixable objections

**Fatal to the plan as stated ("a dozen, now"):**
1. **It solves a problem the data doesn't show.** About 6 owners have been reached and none has logged a trust objection. Spending the trial's remaining capacity on a trust fix before measuring trust is fatal to the plan's purpose, which is learning whether PVD Sites is viable. Twelve free deals inside a 39-day trial also make the trial unreadable.
2. **It crowds out the selling.** Revised in round 2 with Tom's figure of about 20 hours a week, about 110 hours left (context.md, "Added during the meeting"). The builds are automated; delivery is not. Delivery means onboarding, chasing assets, the production build and AEOTester, the domain, Cloudflare and a four-platform listing cleanup.
   - The team's per-site guesses put a dozen sites at:
     - 78 h (range 48 to 120) at the CFO's 6.5 h each;
     - 60 to 85 h at ops' 5 to 7 h;
     - 24 to 48 h at pricing's 2 to 4 h.
   - That is 22 to 109% of the remaining hours, most likely about 55 to 75%.
   - So a dozen is **not impossible on paper**, which corrects my round-1 framing. What it does is leave about 30 to 50 hours to sell 96 qualified deals, follow up the referred leads, and deliver any paid site, in December.
   - It is fatal at a dozen because of what Tom gives up for it, not because the hours don't exist. At 3 sites (12 to 30 h) it is affordable.

**Fixable, each with its fix:**
- **Price anchoring among neighbors.** Fix: give the free sites to community groups (docs/free-sites.md, Greenfield Girls Softball League first), not to businesses. Or call them "founding client" sites at a disclosed discount with a written reason, never "free".
- **Who counts as networked is undefined.** Fix: a written selection rule using objective signals (sponsors a league or event, holds an association or chamber office, or appears in another business's listing or posts), checked against the "no or weak site" rule before anyone is picked.
- **The scope gets said aloud and never written down (the Burr Yacht Sales precedent, T-143, T-261).** Fix: a one-page written scope listing what is included from the D-1971 list and what is not (domain cost, care, changes after launch). Ask for introductions, never Google reviews (D-1975). Send it before any work starts.
- **No referral tracking.** Fix: record each introduction as an Open Brain relation (the CRM has a `referrer` relation type) with the date, plus a "referred by" line on the deal, before the first introduction arrives. Ops owns this.
- **The credit line cannot ship.** The production check fails any page that carries the studio name (scripts/check-production.mjs line 148). Fix: a per-lead flag allowing a credit line for free or community sites only, with a test.
- **FTC disclosure.** Under the Endorsement Guides (16 CFR 255, revised 2023), a free service is a material connection. A testimonial from a free-site owner used in PVD Sites marketing must disclose that the site was free. Fix: put "site provided free" next to any such quote. Legal should confirm.
- **No trigger for collecting assets.** D-1951 and D-1953 key off payment. Fix: make "signed scope" the trigger for free or deferred-payment deals.

### What evidence would change my mind
- **Trust objections in the log.** At least 15 to 20 owners reached through the full 5-touch sequence (D-2050), objections logged word for word, and "don't know you / is this legit / pay a stranger?" turning out to be the most common one. That would make me support 2 or 3 free or founding sites as proof of work.
- **Twelve real, named, networked owners.** If Tom can name 12 owners who meet the "no or weak site" rule and also have an objective network signal, assumption 2 holds.
- **Referrals that need a gift.** If the warm owners are asked for an introduction now, with no gift, and decline, while a gift-backed ask in a small pilot produces introductions, then assumption 3 holds. If they refer for nothing, as Madison Hart did, the gift is wasted money.
- **A pilot that pays.** If 2 free sites produce 2 or more paying referred customers within 30 days of going live, I'd back expanding toward a dozen.

**The cheapest test** takes one week, costs $0 and needs no new build:
1. **Finish the warm follow-ups.** Run touches 2 to 5 on the 5 or 6 warm owners (Lobanton, Still Fly, Ale House, Golden Ridge, Salon Persia, Executive Cuts and Impulse) with a direct close ask, and add a "pay when it's live at your address" option.
2. **Ask for introductions now.** Ask each warm owner for one introduction, with no gift attached.
3. **Log objections word for word** for the next 20 owners reached.
4. **List networked candidates.** Spend 2 hours listing them using the objective signals above.
5. **Apply the decision rule.** If trust tops the objection log and the 2-hour list reaches 12 or more names, bring a pilot of 2 or 3 free sites back to the board, with written scope and tracking. Otherwise drop the giveaway and fix the offer or the channel the log points to. (Round 2: I would now run any business pilot only after the trial, because of the anchoring point under "Changed after team review". The Greenfield Girls Softball League site can go ahead after the first paid delivery either way.)
