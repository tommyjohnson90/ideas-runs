# Synthesizer: Item 1, free sites for well-networked owners

Board meeting 2026-11-23, PVD Sites (`business-websites`). Sources: brief.md, context.md, current-state.md, motions.md (M1.1 to M1.13) and all seven files in `items/1-free-sites/findings/`. Every hour figure in this report is a member's guess: no site has been delivered or timed.

(Written by the synthesizer; saved to this file by the lead, because the harness blocked the synthesizer from writing it. Text unchanged.)

## 1. Bottom line

No. Don't give a dozen free sites to business owners, and don't give any to businesses before Jan 1. Every one of the 85 demo popups already says "$250, done, yours", and two owners have been quoted $250 directly. A free site in those clusters creates a second price among neighbours who talk to each other. Meanwhile the evidence doesn't point to the problem a giveaway would fix: about 5 or 6 owners have been reached, nobody has raised a trust objection, and the one real referral (Madison Hart, I-165) came without a gift. Make "free" something every owner can earn instead: they pay $250, and two neighbours who buy pay it back. Spend the free work on up to 2 community groups. Put the rest of your roughly 110 hours into Lobanton, the warm owners and asking for named introductions now.

- **Who to pick and how:** no business owners during the trial. Ask each warm owner which of 3 to 5 neighbours with built demos they know (M1.9b). The free sites go to Greenfield Girls Softball League first, or to Gail Ahlers first if T-209 says you owe her help.
- **What the free site includes and excludes:** the community site gets the D-1971 package, without the review QR card and without the Apple, Yelp and Bing cleanup. The group owns the domain. It has a 30-day window to fix errors and a plain-text credit. No store, no translations, no hosting, no care plan and no ongoing changes.
- **What you ask in return:** requests only (introductions, the credit line, portfolio use, a labelled testimonial). Never a review, an insurance purchase or a quota.
- **How referrals are tracked and rewarded:** a fixed tag in the Open Brain deal title, "(ref: <Referrer>)". The ledger is two interactions, "Referral given" and "Referral paid". A payout next action falls due 7 days after the referred business buys, and an agent reconciles monthly.
- **How it fits $250 and the $50:** record $250 as a decision and hold it through the trial. Open the $50 to anyone who introduces a buyer, and say it at the pitch, not only after launch. The introducer can keep the $50 or give it to the new owner as $50 off. A paying customer gets $125 back on each of their first two referred sales, so their own site ends up free.

## 2. Motions

When reopened, they come back as a pilot of 2 under M1.2's conditions.

**Why:** a second price in clusters can't be undone. Version B's test can be had for $0: M1.9b tests whether owners refer, and M1.10 gives you a live reference site. Hours no longer decide this, because all seven members agree 4 sites fit in 110 hours.

**For A:** contrarian (proposer), pricing, customer advocate. **For B:** CFO (with the amendment that batch 2 waits for one timed paid delivery), ops, customer experience. Legal: either is lawful.

**Against A:** a small gated pilot tests the referral idea at a capped cost of about 10 to 13 hours if the gate fails (CFO).

**On the dozen:** both versions reject it (CFO F2). At about 75 to 80 hours, a dozen is about 70% of the trial (CFO).

### M1.4 The $50 referral: open to anyone, said at the pitch, give-or-get
**Recommend:** approve (a) and (b), as amended by M1.5(c).
- **(a)** Anyone who introduces a buyer earns $50. It is said at the pitch and printed on the card. The card and popup wording changes only on Tom's word.
- **(b)** Give-or-get: the introducer keeps $50 in cash, or the new owner gets $50 off. Log each choice.

**Why:** it costs $0 up front and is paid only out of revenue. Madison Hart qualifies today. Give-or-get puts the benefit on the person being referred, which is the half the research says moves results. The total stays at $50.

**For:** advocate, CFO, pricing, contrarian, customer experience. **Against:** none. Commercial bribery is handled in M1.5(c).

### M1.5 Written referral terms
**Recommend:** approve every part, with two amendments.
- **(a) When it is paid:** after the referred business has paid and its site is live.
- **(b) Who gets paid:** the first written introduction wins, one payout per buying business, and a 90-day attribution window.
- **(c) Staff of the buying business:** by default they get the "give" option ($50 off for their employer). Cash goes to them only with the buying owner's written knowledge, logged. A public official acting in their role is never paid. An association's staff member is paid only if the association allows it. *Amendment:* making "give" the default for staff settles the advocate-versus-pricing clash without Tom having to ask a new buyer "may I pay your stylist?"
- **(d) Records and tax:** every payout and refund logged with a receipt. W-9 at $1,000 to one payee in a year, 1099-NEC at $2,000. A CPA confirms that the $125 refund is a price reduction, not income.
- **(e) End date:** *Amendment:* the offer runs through **2027-06-30**, and rewards already earned are always paid.

**Why:** the first payout is blocked until these terms are written (legal). They remove the double, missed and staff payouts.

**For:** legal, ops (proposers); CFO, advocate, pricing, customer experience. **Against:** none.

### M1.6 Two-tier earn-back ($125, $125, then $50) and the counter line
**Recommend:** approve **version (i), every buyer**, with these rules:
- The $125 refunds go only to the paying buyer. The referred friend's benefit is the same chance to earn their own site back.
- Give-or-get applies to the $50 payments (non-customer introducers, and a buyer's third referral onward).
- CFO's review at v2: keep the $125 tier only if referred sales are at least 30% of closes.
- **(b) The line:** "$250, done, yours. Send me two neighbours who buy and I'll pay you back $125 for each." That is legal's wording. Reject "five neighbours repays"; the advocate withdrew it.
- **(c) "Free" claims:** approve. Any "free" claim carries its condition in the same sentence.

**Why:** this gives Tom what he was asking for, free sites for owners who actually refer, but only after the referrals have paid for it. It is never cash-negative, and one rule for everyone means no favouritism.

**For (i):** CFO, advocate, customer experience. **For (ii), 3 founders only:** pricing, which opposes (i).

**Against:** the contrarian opposes any tier, because the evidence on rewarding the referrer is weak, "though far better than a free site". The advocate opposes (ii) as favouritism. The extra cost is at most $150 per buyer, and only after $500 of referred sales.

### M1.8 Referred businesses and closing the loop
**Recommend:** approve (a) and (b).
- **(a)** A warm sequence: at most 3 touches, the referrer is named in touch 1, the demo is built before that touch, and the preview end is mentioned once.
- **(b)** Two next actions: "Tell <referrer> I've been to see <friend>" 2 days after the friend's first touch, and the payout within 7 days of the friend buying.

**Why:** referrers who hear nothing don't refer twice, and a cold takedown clock embarrasses the person who sent the friend. It costs $0.

**For:** ops, customer experience, advocate. **Against:** none.

## 4. Confidence and gaps

**Rests on zero paid deliveries.** None of the following has been measured:
- the close rate;
- hours per delivery;
- cold-prospecting hours per sale (the CFO's "A");
- whether owners refer;
- whether the $50 or the $125 tier changes anything.

The pipeline is an inventory of demos (85), not of conversations (about 5 or 6). Every break-even figure is low confidence (CFO).

**Guesses:**
- hours per site (4 to 10);
- 8 hours per community site;
- travel time (3 to 4 hours per dedicated round trip);
- domain renewal (about $10 to $15 a year);
- the Venmo business fee (1.9% + $0.10, unverified);
- a lawyer review ($300 to $750);
- the agent-day to build the credit exception.

**Evidence quality.**
- Gershon, Cryder and John (2020) was read through a university summary, not the paper.
- The "18% of free-site recipients" figure is a vendor's own claim.
- The "83% say, 29% do" figure has no traceable source.
- Every owner quote is Tom's note as summarized by the secretary.
- Legal flags several items as from memory, among them state consumer-protection scope, VT and NH contractor rules and DBA rules.

**Medium-high confidence:** no dozen, the price conflict inside clusters, and the tracking design.
**Medium:** that the earn-back beats flat $50 in this market.
**Low:** that community sites produce referrals.
